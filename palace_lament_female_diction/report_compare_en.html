This focused report compares only the two large groups — male-ventriloquized palace-lament (G1m, 256 poems) and all poems by female poets (G2, 214) — so every contrast rests on a substantial sample. Ventriloquism is a genre; the female corpus is authorship-wide, so palace-specific lexis is naturally denser in ventriloquism. The revealing differences are in register.

I

Distinctive terms

Ventriloquism loads on court exempla — 長門 Changmen, 昭陽 Zhaoyang, 金屋 golden house, 團扇 round fan, 飛燕 Feiyan, 棄妾 cast-off wife; female poets on lived palace detail and personal registers — 內人, 畫船, 紅箋, 郎 beloved, 儂 I, 相思 lovesickness.

II

Lexical fields

Ventriloquism is denser in every palace-lament field — most sharply favour 君恩 (244 vs 132, ~1.8×) — the expected genre effect.

III

Syntax & function words

Two register signals stand out. Second-person (君/爾) is ~1.8× higher in ventriloquism (122 vs 67): the ventriloquized lament is structured as a woman’s address to her lord. Conversely, female poets use ~2× more sentence-final particles (兮/矣/乎/哉; 38 vs 18) and strongly prefer the seven-syllable line (68% vs 53%).
